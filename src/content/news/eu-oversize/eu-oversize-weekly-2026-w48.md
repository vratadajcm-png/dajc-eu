## Intro

This European road-freight briefing covers **23–29 November 2026 (ISO week 2026-W48)**.

This edition was prepared after its scheduled release on Friday 20 November 2026 at 12:00 CET. It reports verified changes for 2026-W48; the publication metadata records the delayed preparation, not an on-time release. A temporary Polish legal derogation is in force during the target week for a narrowly defined category of drivers transporting liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) domestically in road tankers. The precise limits, recording duty and safety conditions are laid out in the original government notice. These rules must not be treated as a general exemption for every truck, every hazardous-goods load or international haulage.

## Lead reports

### Poland: temporary driving-time, break and weekly-rest derogation for ADR-qualified domestic LPG tanker drivers

The Polish Minister of Infrastructure issued a notice dated **6 November 2026**, published in *Monitor Polski* as **2026 item 1107**, establishing temporary derogations under Article 14(2) of Regulation (EC) No 561/2006. The measures apply in Poland **from 6 November through 6 December 2026**. The official text limits their application to drivers who hold the relevant **ADR certificate for tank carriage** and perform **domestic road transport of LPG in tankers**. The notice does not establish a blanket exemption for other goods, all vehicles with tachographs or cross-border LPG journeys.

**Category:** Operational development — driving-time and rest rules  
**Where:** Poland; qualifying domestic road transport of LPG in tankers  
**Affected drivers/vehicles:** Drivers with the required ADR tank-carriage qualification engaged in domestic LPG tanker transport. The notice itself does not specify a vehicle-mass threshold; do not infer one.  
**When:** 6 November–6 December 2026, including the target week of 23–29 November

**What changed:** For eligible drivers, the notice provides these temporary limits and conditions:

- **Daily driving:** no more than **11 hours**.
- **Weekly driving:** no more than **60 hours**.
- **Driving over two consecutive weeks:** no more than **96 hours**.
- **Break:** one continuous break of at least **45 minutes after 5.5 hours of driving**.
- **Regular weekly rest:** the driver may choose to take it in a stationary vehicle if suitable sleeping accommodation exists for each driver, as expressly provided by this temporary notice.
- **Tachograph record:** each use of a derogation must be recorded by hand on the reverse of the analogue tachograph chart or of a digital tachograph printout.

**Impact:** Eligible domestic LPG tanker schedules may use the temporary flexibility, but only within all the stated limits and conditions. The notice explicitly says applying these derogations must not worsen drivers' working conditions or road safety. It does **not** amend ordinary restrictions for other transport or establish a special overweight, abnormal-load or route permit.

**Recommended action:** Before planning a Polish domestic LPG tanker movement under the derogation, confirm the driver's ADR tank qualification, the domestic nature of the trip and the precise applicability of the official notice. Use the **11 h / 60 h / 96 h** ceilings together with the **45-minute break after 5.5 hours**; comply with the handwritten tachograph-record requirement, the rest conditions and the safety safeguard. Do not extend this exception to international movements, other loads or other drivers without a separate legal basis. Revert to the normally applicable provisions after 6 December 2026 unless an official extension is published.

**Exemptions/conditions:** This is a limited derogation from specified provisions of Regulation (EC) No 561/2006, not a general relaxation of EU drivers' hours rules. The official notice provides no new general HGV driving ban.

- Poland: Confirm both ADR tank qualification and domestic LPG tanker scope before applying the temporary derogation; respect the daily, weekly, two-week, break, rest and tachograph-record conditions exactly; verify whether any official extension exists before planning beyond 6 December.
